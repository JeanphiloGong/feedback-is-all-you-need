# Seed idea

## Working thesis

Current AI research often treats intelligence as a scaling problem: increase
parameters, data, and training compute, then expect a sufficiently capable
system to emerge. This project proposes a different emphasis.

After a model reaches a minimum capacity for representing language and the
world, intelligence may be produced mainly by a closed loop that connects
prediction, action, real outcomes, error attribution, memory, and policy
revision. The model does not need to contain every useful fact or strategy in
its fixed parameters if it can repeatedly acquire, test, compress, and reuse
them through interaction.

Books and other cultural artifacts can be viewed as externalized memory: they
compress the results of many people’s attempts, failures, discoveries, and
social corrections. Human intelligence is therefore plausibly a property of a
larger brain–body–tool–culture feedback system.

## Strong claim to test

> Once a model has crossed a minimum representational threshold, increasing the
> quality and efficiency of its feedback loop produces more transferable
> intelligence per unit of compute than increasing parameter count alone.

## Important boundary

“Small model” refers to the learned controller or cognitive core. The complete
agent may include external memory, tools, simulators, evaluators, and a social
or physical environment. Experiments must report these components separately
so that system-level intelligence is not confused with model-only capacity.
